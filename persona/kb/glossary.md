# Acme Insurance Glossary

Plain-language definitions Sam can reach for. Always paraphrase to match the
caller's vocabulary; never assume they know these terms.

## General

**Premium** — The amount the policyholder pays Acme (monthly or yearly) for
the policy. Comes out of their pocket whether they file a claim or not.

**Deductible** — The portion of a covered loss the policyholder pays before
Acme starts paying. A $500 collision deductible means Acme covers everything
above $500. Higher deductibles → lower premiums.

**Limit** — The maximum Acme pays for a covered loss. A $250k bodily-injury
liability limit means Acme will pay up to $250k for injuries to other people
in an accident the insured caused.

**Peril** — The cause of damage (collision, fire, theft, vandalism, wind,
hail, water, etc.). Policies cover named perils; if a peril isn't listed in
the policy's covered perils, it's not covered.

**Covered loss** — A loss that (1) was caused by a covered peril and (2)
happened during the active policy period. Both must be true.

**Adjuster** — The Acme employee who investigates the claim, inspects damage
if needed, and determines the final payout. Sam handles intake; adjusters
handle resolution.

**Subrogation** — When Acme pays the policyholder for a loss caused by
someone else, then chases that other person (or their insurer) for
reimbursement. The policyholder doesn't have to do anything for this.

## Auto

**Collision** — Damage from hitting another vehicle, an object (curb, pole,
guardrail), or rolling over. Optional coverage on most policies.

**Comprehensive** — Damage from anything that isn't a collision: theft,
vandalism, weather (hail, falling tree), fire, hitting an animal, broken
glass. Optional coverage.

**Liability — bodily injury** — Pays for medical bills + lost wages + pain
and suffering for OTHER PEOPLE the insured injured in an accident they
caused. Required in most states.

**Liability — property damage** — Pays for damage the insured did to
OTHER PEOPLE's property (their car, their fence, their mailbox). Required.

**Uninsured/underinsured motorist (UM/UIM)** — Pays for the insured's
injuries when the at-fault driver has no insurance or not enough.

**Rental reimbursement** — Pays for a rental car while the insured's car is
being repaired after a covered loss. Has a daily cap and a maximum number
of days.

## Home

**Dwelling (Coverage A)** — Covers the structure of the house itself.

**Other structures (Coverage B)** — Covers detached structures: garage,
shed, fence.

**Personal property (Coverage C)** — Covers belongings inside the home
(furniture, clothes, electronics). Usually limited as a percentage of
dwelling coverage.

**Loss of use (Coverage D)** — Pays for additional living expenses (hotel,
restaurant meals) while the home is uninhabitable due to a covered loss.

**Liability (Coverage E)** — Pays if someone is injured on the property
and the homeowner is found liable.

**Actual Cash Value (ACV)** — Replacement cost minus depreciation. A 10-year-old
TV is paid out at what a 10-year-old TV is worth today, not what a new
equivalent costs.

**Replacement Cost Value (RCV)** — Pays what it costs today to replace the
item with a new equivalent. More expensive coverage.

**Mitigation** — Reasonable steps the policyholder takes to prevent further
damage after a loss (turning off water main after a pipe burst, covering a
hole in the roof with a tarp). Acme reimburses reasonable mitigation costs.

## Renters

**Personal property** — Same idea as homeowners Coverage C. Covers belongings
in a rented unit.

**Theft** — Stolen property. Police report is usually required for the
claim to proceed.

**Loss of use** — Hotel and meal coverage if the unit becomes uninhabitable
due to a covered loss.

**Liability** — Pays if a guest is injured in the rented unit.

## What Acme does NOT cover (common)

- Flood damage from rising surface water (separate NFIP policy required)
- Earthquake (separate California Earthquake Authority policy required)
- Wear and tear, gradual leaks, mold from long-term moisture
- Damage from acts of war
- Intentional damage by the insured

If a caller's loss falls into one of these, say so plainly, sympathetically,
and offer to escalate to a human if they want to discuss options.
